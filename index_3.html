Universities Business Challenge

Year 1:

I joined one of the 3 UoD teams entered in the Univerities Business Challenge in my first semester. We started strong in the first round, where 8 teams from around the UK competed over a 6 week period to run a simulated business, trying to generate as much profit as possible while also building and maintaining a strong balance sheet. The simulation had great depth, which involved social externalities. For example, we had to keep costs low, while also paying fair wages and pricing responsibly as there was a reputation metric that would be taken into account and effect future sales. We were very successful in this stage and quickly gained first place and held it for the duration of the round, finishing with around 25% higher profits than second place qualifying us for the second round along with around 30 of the original 125 teams.

The second round would be the semi-finals, there were 3 semi-finals held across the UK. We were in the Edinburgh Semi-finals and competed with a range of Universiti including ones from England. The second round definitely had a more immersive side to it, as on top of having to complete a similar business simulation once again measured by profit, there were also points to be won through more qualitative metrics as teams had to build impromptu marketing campaigns and adverts. Once again we did very well placing 4th unfortunately it was the top 3 teams that would qualify for the finals, I am not t proud to admit that I'm still stung by missing the cut by only 0.07% on the points.

Year 2:

It's clear just how much I enjoyed the challenge as I immediately signed up for it again in my 2nd year, this time as team leader. I was teamed up with 3 others, 2 of them being 4th years. This was a great experience as it taught me how to lead and motivate people, how to delegate, and how to handle negative situations that arise between members. We worked well and quickly began making profits from the first week. I also made two very good friends from this challenge who I still keep up with even as they have graduated.

Year 3:

In my 3rd year I knew I needed to use my time at university to explore more things, especially after experiencing the UBC through both the perspective of team member and leader, and having the achievement of representing my university in national semi-finals. However, I still felt very close to the challenge. So, after talking to our university's representative of the challenge, I decided to take on a mentor style role for my 3rd year. Where I introduced the new teams to the challenge during a presentation and spoke to them about what they should expect to see throughout its duration. I let them know they could ask me for help on any of the 'decisions', as it could be tricky to decipher exactly what you were being asked to do, especially on the first go. It was just to help the students, especially non-business ones to understand their objectives a little easier, to help prevent any unnecessary mistakes, so they could make the most of the challenge. I look forward to doing this again in my 4th year.
